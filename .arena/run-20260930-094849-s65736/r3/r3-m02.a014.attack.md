ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The recommended subject can suggest Illan was at the salon, and the solution wrongly says grammar rules this out
Where: Section 2, subject no. 1 "Colette, rencontrée à South World Wines, m'a parlé de votre mission", its justification ("c'est donc Colette que vous avez rencontrée au salon, et l'objet ne rattache au salon ni Illan ni sa mission"), the claim "Aucun ne suppose qu'Illan était au salon", and Section 4 point 7 ("c'est une question de grammaire").
Problem: The feminine agreement only shows that Colette is the person who was met. It does not show who met her. The past participle has no agent, and the subject line is addressed to Illan. So "Colette, rencontrée à South World Wines" reads just as naturally as "Colette, [que vous avez] rencontrée à South World Wines". A concrete case: Illan was not at the salon. He sees this subject in his inbox and thinks "I never met a Colette at a wine fair", then treats the mail as a mistake or as spam. Neither the subject nor the preview says that the sender is the one who met her. The solution presents as settled by grammar something that is actually ambiguous. That breaks constraint 4 ("Signaler explicitement toute incertitude plutôt que de présenter une affirmation non vérifiée comme un fait"), and it is the default subject the user is told to send.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The consent problem is flagged, but there is nothing to do if Colette refuses or cannot be reached
Where: Section 4 point 2: "Le contexte ne dit pas si Colette a accepté d'être citée [...] Si vous avez un doute, prévenez-la."
Problem: The body opens with Colette, and the default subject (no. 1) and subject no. 2 both lead with her name. The solution admits her consent is unknown, but its only instruction is "prévenez-la". It does not say what to do if she says no, if she does not reply, or if the user cannot reach her before sending. It does not name a subject to fall back on in that case, and it does not say whether the body can go out at all without her. On the scenario it raises itself (consent in doubt), the user has to guess. The safe default, which is to send under her name, is also the one that exposes her.

ATTACK 3 [MINOR] Colette's surname is treated as missing, but the body has no bracketed placeholder for it
Where: Section 4 point 2: "Si le prénom et le salon risquent de ne pas suffire, ajoutez son nom ou son entreprise dans le corps." The body only says "Colette, que j'ai rencontrée...".
Problem: Constraint 1 says "Là où une information manque [...] laisser un emplacement entre crochets". The solution itself says Colette's surname or company may be needed, yet the body has no [Nom de Colette] or [Entreprise de Colette] slot, and the note does not say where in the sentence to put it. The user has to work out the wording and the position.

ATTACK 4 [MINOR] The year 2026 is stated as fact when it is an assumption
Where: Section 4 point 5: "Le 21 septembre 2026 est bien un lundi."
Problem: The context gives "lundi 21 septembre" with no year. The solution checks the weekday against 2026 and calls this a verification, but it never says that the salon taking place in 2026 is an assumption (it is not in Section 5, Hypothèses either). If the salon was in another year, "lundi 21 septembre" is wrong, and the "check" would have hidden that. Constraint 4 asks for this kind of assumption to be flagged.
