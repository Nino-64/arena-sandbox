ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The "de la part de" variant, applied as instructed, produces a self-contradictory opening paragraph
Where: Section 1, "**Variante de la première phrase.** ... « Je vous contacte de la part de Colette, que j'ai rencontrée lundi 21 septembre au salon South World Wines et qui m'a parlé de votre mission d'Hospitality Manager chez Have a Good Day. »"
Problem: The variant is labelled as a replacement for the *first sentence* only, so the second sentence of the paragraph stays. Followed literally, the opening becomes: « Je vous contacte de la part de Colette, que j'ai rencontrée [...] et qui m'a parlé de votre mission [...]. C'est ce qui m'a donné l'idée de vous écrire. » That says "I am writing on Colette's behalf" and then "this is what gave me the idea to write to you", so it claims both that Colette sent him and that it was his own idea. It also says twice that he is writing. The solution never says to delete « C'est ce qui m'a donné l'idée de vous écrire » when using the variant. This is the exact case (Colette did ask him to reach out) where the user is told to use the variant, so the flaw shows up in a supported path, not in a rare edge case.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The recommended subject line implies that Colette is affiliated with South World Wines, which the context never states
Where: Section 2, object 1 « Colette (South World Wines) : votre offre café chez Have a Good Day », marked « Recommandé »
Problem: In a subject line or signature, the convention "Prénom (Organisation)" reads as the person's employer or affiliation, as in « Colette (Danone) ». The context only says that Colette was *met at* the South World Wines trade show. It says nothing about her working for it or representing it. This breaks constraint 1 ("Ne rien inventer") and constraint 4 (no unverified claim presented as fact), and it does so in the subject the solution recommends as its first choice. The self-check under the table (« Aucun objet ne décrit la machine au-delà de ce que dit le contexte ») only looks at claims about the machine, so it misses this implied claim about a person. Object 2 (« Suite à ma rencontre avec Colette au salon South World Wines ») says the same thing without the ambiguity, which shows the problem was avoidable.

ATTACK 3 [MINOR] Object 4 uses the "A × B" notation, which signals an existing partnership that does not exist
Where: Section 2, object 4 « Have a Good Day × L'Arbre à Café : explorer des synergies ? »
Problem: In marketing French and English, « Marque × Marque » is the standard way to announce a collaboration or a co-branded offer. Putting Have a Good Day's name next to the sender's with « × » in a first cold email suggests a joint project that the context does not support (constraint 1). A skim reader may see it as presumptuous or misleading, even with the question mark. The solution flags nothing about this object.

ATTACK 4 [MINOR] No usable fallback if Colette cannot be reached or refuses to be named
Where: Section 4, point 3: « Si vous n'êtes pas sûr qu'elle accepte d'être citée auprès d'Illan, prévenez-la avant l'envoi. »
Problem: The solution raises the consent uncertainty (constraint 4) but gives only one action: warn her. It says nothing about the two likely outcomes: she cannot be reached before sending, or she says no. The body has no version without Colette, since both the default and the variant name her in the first sentence. The objects that do not name her (3, 4, 5) are never marked as the ones to use in that case. A user whose Colette says "please don't mention me" has to rewrite the opening on their own. Point 3 also asks the user to check her consent and her "de la part de" status separately, but gives no combined decision (which body, which subject) for the four possible cases.

ATTACK 5 [MINOR] The link is framed as prep for a call that has not been agreed, so the document loses its purpose if Illan does not say yes
Where: Section 1, body, after the call to action: « Si vous souhaitez vous faire une idée avant notre échange, voici la présentation de La Reniala R1 »
Problem: « avant notre échange » treats the call as already agreed. The paragraph comes right after the question asking whether Illan is available, so it contradicts that open question. It also ties the only reason to open the link to a meeting Illan has not accepted: if he is unsure, the email gives him no reason to open the presentation. The solution's own table says the link is « présenté comme une lecture facultative », but the wording makes it conditional on a yes. Constraint 2 is met, but the link is less useful in the most likely case, a hesitant recipient.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] The instructions for the time-slot placeholders do not tell the user which dates to pick
Where: Section 4, point 7: « Mettez deux dates et heures réelles, les jours ouvrés qui suivent l'envoi (jour, date, heure). »
Problem: « les jours ouvrés qui suivent l'envoi » has no minimum or maximum delay. Read literally, it lets the user offer tomorrow morning to a stranger, which is too soon to be credible for a cold contact and likely to be missed before Illan even reads the email. It also lets them offer dates a month away. It says nothing about public holidays. The user has to guess the window, and [créneau 1] and [créneau 2] are the two placeholders that decide whether the call to action can be answered with one word, as the solution itself claims (« Illan peut répondre en un mot »).
