ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The uncertainty table doubts a fact the brief states, and uses that doubt to reword the objective
Where: Section 4, row "Existence d'une offre café et d'espaces de bureau chez Have a Good Day": "Le contexte ne décrit pas l'activité de Have a Good Day. Le message parle donc de « comment le café est proposé » sans supposer de dispositif précis." In the body: "j'aimerais d'abord comprendre comment le café est proposé aujourd'hui chez Have a Good Day".
Problem: The brief states the objective plainly: "mieux connaître l'offre café actuelle d'Illan". So the brief does establish that a current coffee offer exists. Listing its existence as an uncertainty is wrong, and constraint 4 asks for real uncertainties to be flagged, not for invented doubts about given facts. Because of that doubt, the body drops the brief's wording "offre café actuelle" and shifts the subject from Illan's offer to "comment le café est proposé chez Have a Good Day". The rewording is also inconsistent: object 3 still says "Votre offre café chez Have a Good Day", which assumes the very offer the table treats as uncertain.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The body offers the presentation link and then says the product won't be presented
Where: Body: "Sa présentation est disponible ici : [lien] / Plutôt que de vous la présenter en détail par écrit, j'aimerais d'abord comprendre..." and Section 3 point 5: "la présentation du produit devient facultative."
Problem: Constraint 2 asks for the product presentation link to be part of the message. The very next sentence says "plutôt que de vous la présenter", which tells Illan he doesn't need to look at the link, and Section 3 says so outright ("devient facultative"). The link is technically there, but the text next to it pushes the reader away from it. The two sentences also contradict each other: the product has just been presented by link, and the next line says it won't be.

ATTACK 3 [MINOR] An unverified assumption is stated as fact in the reasoning for the default subject
Where: Section 2: "Pourquoi l'objet 1 par défaut : dans un message non sollicité, citer une personne que le destinataire connaît augmente probablement le taux d'ouverture..."
Problem: This reasoning assumes Illan knows Colette. The context never says so. It only says Colette spoke to the sender about Illan's mission. The solution's own table 4 admits it doesn't know what Colette said to Illan, and the note on object 2 says "Si Illan connaît bien Colette". The main recommendation therefore depends on an assumption that isn't flagged where it is used, which goes against constraint 4 ("Signaler explicitement toute incertitude").

ATTACK 4 [MINOR] "Conçue pour" is not the context's wording, but Section 3 says it is
Where: Body: "La Reniala R1, une machine conçue pour les espaces de bureau". Section 3 point 7: "« Conçue pour les espaces de bureau » et « dédiée aux espaces de bureau » reprennent le contexte."
Problem: The context says "dédiée aux espaces de bureau". "Conçue pour" adds a claim about how the machine was designed, which the context does not make. The change is small, but Section 3 says the wording is the context's own, and it is not. Constraint 1 forbids adding features.

ATTACK 5 [MINOR] The pre-send checklist misses placeholders inside the body
Where: Body: "par exemple [jour et heure – option 1] ou [jour et heure – option 2]". Section 4 table, row "Signature": only "Fonction, téléphone, e-mail et site".
Problem: Section 4 is the checklist the user runs before sending, and it lists only the signature placeholders. The two time-slot placeholders sit in the middle of the body and appear only in Section 3's explanations. A user who follows the checklist can send the email with "[jour et heure – option 1]" still in it, which would damage the message far more than a missing phone number.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] Object 6 is too long, so its useful part gets cut off
Where: Object 6: "L'Arbre à Café et Have a Good Day : Colette m'a parlé de votre mission"
Problem: This subject is about 70 characters. A typical mobile inbox shows the first 35 to 45, so the reader sees "L'Arbre à Café et Have a Good Day : Col…". The name the table relies on ("Colette") disappears, and what remains puts the two companies side by side as if they already had a relationship, which is not the case for a first contact.
