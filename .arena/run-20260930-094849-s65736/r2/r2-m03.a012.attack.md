ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The "offre café existante" fix fixes nothing and skips the subject lines
Where: Section 4, point 6: « Si ce n'est pas certain, remplacez « votre offre café actuelle » par « la façon dont vous gérez aujourd'hui l'offre café ». » Also subjects 3 « Illan, un court échange sur votre offre café ? » and 4 « Votre offre café chez Have a Good Day ».
Problem: The solution names the uncertainty (Have a Good Day may have no coffee offer) but its replacement, « la façon dont vous gérez aujourd'hui l'offre café », still assumes an offer exists and that Illan manages it. The definite article « l' » makes the same claim again. Counterexample: Have a Good Day has no coffee offer. The user applies the fix exactly as written and still sends a message that assumes one. Subjects 3 and 4 also carry the assumption, and point 6 does not tell the user to change them. So a user who follows the advice can end up with a corrected body and an uncorrected subject line. Constraint 4 (« Signaler explicitement toute incertitude plutôt que de présenter une affirmation non vérifiée comme un fait ») is only half met: the uncertainty is flagged, but the fix it offers keeps the unverified claim.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] It states unverified claims as facts, then its self-check says everything was flagged
Where: Section 5, first bullet: « Ils pointent vers votre propre domaine, ce qui est habituel dans un mail professionnel » and « c'est une précaution courante ». Section 6, last row: « Tout ajout qui ne vient pas du contexte est signalé comme hypothèse (format, année, créneaux) — Réussi ».
Problem: The context says nothing about L'Arbre à Café having its own domain, a website or a company email address. Yet the solution states as fact that the signature links « pointent vers votre propre domaine ». Counterexample: the sender uses a personal webmail address and has no website. The spam-filter reasoning then rests on a false premise, and « [Site web] » is a placeholder for something that may not exist (it is not marked « si existant »). Its anti-spam advice (« précaution courante ») is also general knowledge stated as fact. This breaks constraint 4. The section 6 table then marks « Réussi » on exactly this criterion, so the user is told the check passed when it did not.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] Colette appears by first name only, with no placeholder and no warning
Where: Body: « Colette, que j'ai rencontrée le lundi 21 septembre… », subjects 1 and 2, and all of section 4.
Problem: The solution calls the recommendation « votre meilleur atout », then gives Illan only a first name to identify the person behind it. Constraint 1 says « Là où une information manque […], laisser un emplacement entre crochets ». Colette's surname (or her company) is missing information that matters here, yet no [Nom de Colette] placeholder is provided. Constraint 4 asks for uncertainties to be flagged, yet none of the nine points in section 4 raises the risk that Illan will not know which Colette is meant. Counterexample: Illan knows several people named Colette, or met her only briefly. « Colette m'a parlé de votre mission » then reads like a cold approach, or even phishing, and the recommendation stops working.

ATTACK 4 [MINOR] The runtime clock is presented as the send date, and tool internals leak into the deliverable
Where: Section 4, point 9: « nous sommes le 30 septembre 2026 (vérifié avec la commande `date`). […] envoyé cette semaine, « lundi dernier » désignerait le 28 septembre ».
Problem: The context never says when the email will be sent. The clock of the machine that generated the answer does not verify that date, yet the text presents it as a fact (« vérifié »), which breaks constraint 4. A reference to a shell command also means nothing to the user. The advice survives for the wrong reason: the full date is right because the send date is unknown, not because today is 30 September. If the user sends it on 22 September, « lundi dernier » is correct and the stated rationale is wrong.

ATTACK 5 [MINOR] The « prêt à envoyer » message is inside a Markdown blockquote
Where: Section 1: every line of the message starts with « > », including « > https://drive.google.com/file/d/1kzw_QZuyC6rh5HhsTj0-KUlAV1iyAFT5/view ».
Problem: The solution calls the body « prêt à envoyer », but in the raw file (the deliverable as written) each line carries a « > » prefix and blank lines appear as « > ». A user who copies the raw text, or pastes it into a plain-text mail client, sends a message full of quote markers, including one in front of the one link the task requires. There is no plain copy-ready version and no note telling the user to remove the markers.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] The slot placeholders are too vague to fill without guessing
Where: « Je vous propose [créneau 1] ou [créneau 2] » and the checklist « Informations manquantes laissées entre crochets ».
Problem: The placeholders do not say what to write: a date, a time, a duration, or a phone or video link. They also do not tell the user that any duration they give is their own choice and not from the context. The user has to guess the expected format (« mardi 6 octobre à 10 h » ? « la semaine prochaine » ?). An inconsistent fill (« [créneau 1] » replaced with « mardi » only) makes the specific request that section 3 promises (« Deux créneaux à compléter ») vague again, and brings back the open question it criticizes in the base message.
