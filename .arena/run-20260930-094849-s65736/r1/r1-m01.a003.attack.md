ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The recommended subject line implies facts the context does not support
Where: Section 2, subject 1 (the "recommandé" one): « Colette (South World Wines) : votre offre café »
Problem: A name followed by an organisation in parentheses normally means "person (their employer)". Illan will likely read it as "Colette from South World Wines". The context only says the sender met Colette at the South World Wines salon, not that she works there. The pattern "Colette … : votre offre café" also suggests Colette talked about Illan's coffee offer, but per the context she only talked about his mission. This goes against constraint 4 ("Signaler explicitement toute incertitude plutôt que de présenter une affirmation non vérifiée comme un fait"). It also contradicts the solution's own uncertainty point 1 ("Le message ne prétend pas qu'elle vous envoie"): putting Colette's name first in the subject is exactly what makes a referral claim. The "Ce qu'il sacrifie" column does not flag this risk, and the user is told to pick this subject ("Mon choix : l'objet 1").

ATTACK 2 [MINOR] The product name is altered in the body and written inconsistently
Where: Body: « avons développé la Reniala R1 » ; subject 5: « La Reniala R1 »
Problem: The context gives the product name as « La Reniala R1 », with a capital "La" as part of the proper name. The body lowercases it to « la Reniala R1 », so "La" reads as a plain article, while subject 5 keeps « La Reniala R1 ». Constraint 1 forbids altering product names, and the user gets two spellings of the same product in one deliverable. Correct form: « avons développé La Reniala R1 ».

ATTACK 3 [MINOR] The phone/video format is added without being flagged as an assumption
Where: Body: « Seriez-vous disponible pour un court échange, par téléphone ou en visio ? » and the fallback phrase under the message
Problem: The context gives no format for the exchange. The solution adds one but does not list it in "Points à vérifier avant l'envoi" or in "Hypothèses", even though constraint 4 asks for every addition not taken from the context to be flagged. The solution does flag other added items (créneaux, fonction), so this omission is inconsistent. If Nino only wants to meet in person, or only by phone, the message commits him to something he did not choose.

ATTACK 4 [MINOR] The "only one link" anti-spam argument is contradicted by the solution's own signature
Where: Section 5: « Un seul lien, sans raccourcisseur d'URL, limite ce risque. » vs signature « [Téléphone] · [Adresse e-mail] · [Site web] »
Problem: The signature adds a website placeholder and an email address. Once filled in, most mail clients turn these into links, so the message sent will not contain "un seul lien". The mitigation the user is told to rely on does not hold for the message as delivered.

ATTACK 5 [MINOR] An unsupported inference is stated as fact inside the uncertainty list
Where: Uncertainty point 6: « "Hospitality Manager", "Illan" et "Have a Good Day" sont repris du contexte, qui vient lui-même de ce que Colette vous a dit. »
Problem: The context does not say that Illan's name, the company name or the job title came from Colette. It only says she talked about his mission. So the uncertainty section itself states something unverified as fact, which is the thing constraint 4 forbids. Point 7 adds a similar unstated premise, "Le lundi 21 septembre 2026": the task gives no year. Its advice to "garder la date explicite plutôt que « lundi dernier »" also cannot be acted on, because the body already uses the explicit date.
