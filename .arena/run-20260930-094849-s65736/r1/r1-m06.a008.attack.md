ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] Subject line 2 states as fact the very thing the answer itself flags as unverified
Where: Objets proposés, n° 2 « Votre offre café chez Have a Good Day », against À vérifier : « le contexte ne confirme pas que Have a Good Day ait déjà une offre café. Si vous n'en êtes pas sûr, remplacez-la par « la façon dont le café est géré aujourd'hui chez vous » ».
Problem: Constraint 4 says: « Signaler explicitement toute incertitude plutôt que de présenter une affirmation non vérifiée comme un fait. » The answer sees the uncertainty but gives the fallback wording only for the body. Subject n° 2 is a bare statement that an « offre café » exists, and it is the first thing Illan reads. Counterexample: Have a Good Day has no coffee offer, and the user follows the advice and edits the body. The mail then goes out under subject n° 2, which still asserts the unverified premise. No warning covers the subject lines and no alternative subject is given.

ATTACK 2 [MINOR] Subject line 4 implies a partnership that the context does not support, with no warning
Where: Objets proposés, n° 4 « L'Arbre à Café × Have a Good Day ».
Problem: The « A × B » format is the usual way to announce a collaboration or co-branding. The context only says the goal is to « explorer de potentielles synergies ». There is no existing relationship. Constraint 1 forbids any « promesse absent du contexte », and constraint 4 asks for uncertainty to be flagged. The answer's À vérifier section covers the link, the « offre café » wording, Colette, the call format and the date, but says nothing about this subject line. As written, a cold first contact reads like an announced partnership between the two companies.

ATTACK 3 [MINOR] A time frame was added to the request and is the one addition not flagged
Where: Body: « Auriez-vous 15 minutes dans les prochains jours pour un court échange, par téléphone ou en visio ? » against À vérifier: « « 15 minutes, par téléphone ou en visio ». C'est une proposition de format, pas un fait. »
Problem: The answer flags its invented duration and format as the sender's own proposals. It does not flag « dans les prochains jours », which is also absent from the context. That time frame is a commitment the user may not be able to meet (travel, a busy period), and the answer never says to check it. This is the same kind of addition treated two different ways, so the list of assumptions to check before sending is incomplete.

Where: Ce qui a changé: « Le message reste plus court que l'original (90 mots contre 95) tout en ajoutant le lien, les noms et la signature. »
Problem: This is a checkable factual claim. Up to « Bien cordialement, », the rewritten body has about 88–90 words. The signature block the answer adds (« Nino MAISON / [Fonction] / L'Arbre à Café / [Téléphone] · [Adresse e-mail] ») adds 9 more, for about 97–99 in total. The original has 94–95 with its sign-off. With the signature that the sentence itself names, the new message is longer than the original, not shorter. A figure presented as verified is wrong.

ATTACK 5 [MINOR] It says the referral is not in the context, when the context labels it « Recommandation », and it gives no way to restore it
Where: Ce qui a changé: « « De la part de Colette » est retiré. L'expression laisse entendre que Colette vous a demandé de le contacter, ce que le contexte ne dit pas. »
Problem: The context line is headed « Recommandation : Colette… ». Saying the context does not suggest a referral goes too far, and the answer drops the referral framing without warning. À vérifier only says to check that Colette « est d'accord pour être citée ». It gives no wording to use if Colette did recommend the contact, for example « Colette m'a conseillé de vous contacter ». A sender whose referral is real is left with the weaker version and no instruction on how to put back the strongest hook in a cold email.
